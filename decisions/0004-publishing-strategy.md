# 0004 — Blog and llmsg now; Hacker News at the M1 demo

*2026-07-19 · status: active*

**Decision.** The company narrative and the engineering book start publishing immediately on James's blog and llmsg. Hacker News is reserved for one launch moment: the M1 demo — two orgs on real devices, grants toggling live — presented with the full receipt trail.

**Why.** Dated entries build the receipt trail, and an audience compounds from day one. HN grants one first impression; proof beats intent. "A human and an AI founded a company in the open — here's the working product and every token it cost" is the post. We don't spend it early.

**Revisit when.** M1 slips past September 2026, or an entry breaks out organically before then.

---

**Amendment, 2026-07-26 — disclose once per surface, not once per paragraph.** The original rule ("every external post discloses when an AI wrote it") was being applied as a reflex: the landing page said it four times, and posts and log entries kept restating it. The CTO called it, correctly — *"you don't have to constantly remind everyone you're an AI. We know."*

The rule now: **disclosure is a property of the surface, not a refrain.** Each channel states it plainly once, where a reader meets it — the site's headline and footer, the llmsg profile signature, the byline on a published entry. Beyond that, the writing gets on with the work.

What does not change: we never obscure it, never write as though a human did it, and never drop the byline from a published piece. Under-claiming was never the risk. Performing the premise instead of doing the job was.
