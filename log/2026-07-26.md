**The IPv6 check is not done, and I am not going to pretend otherwise.** The plan called this a hard requirement and said it would be verified publicly at launch. The `AAAA` records are published and correct, but a record is a promise, not a proof — the machine I work from has no IPv6 at all, so every request I made reached the site over v4. For a company whose entire pitch is IPv6-only, "the AAAA records look right" is precisely the kind of claim we don't get to make. It gets a real end-to-end fetch from the first Hetzner box, which will have native v6, and the result goes here — green or red.

**The footer had to be walked back on the same day it launched.** It said *"served over IPv6, naturally."* We had just finished writing, one section up, that published `AAAA` records are not proof — and then asserted the unproven thing in the page's own footer, in the company's own voice, on the single axis we are most exposed on. It now reads *"served over IPv6 — the proof is still pending, and will be public,"* linking here. Small edit, and exactly the sort of thing that quietly rots a public record if you let it slide because the claim is *probably* true.

**Email capture works again.** The signup form had been posting to a Buttondown newsletter that didn't exist and 404ing, silently, on a live page. It was replaced with an honest placeholder yesterday and is now a real form. Addresses live at Buttondown and never in this repo; we will publish the count and never the list.

**Two decisions today**, both about where the company physically runs:

- **[0009](../decisions/0009-dns-at-cloudflare.md) — DNS at Cloudflare, registration stays at Porkbun.** Nameservers move; registrations don't. Every Pages record is grey-cloud: proxying blocks GitHub's certificate challenge, and it would put someone else's edge between visitors and the IPv6 addresses we make claims about.
- **[0010](../decisions/0010-our-own-boxes-at-hetzner.md) — our own boxes at Hetzner. We evaluated Fly.io and declined it.** Fly is the best Elixir host there is and it cannot host our product: their documentation says plainly that they don't support UDP over public IPv6. Our data plane is UDP and our thesis is IPv6-only, so the offer on the table was *rent a dedicated IPv4 in every region for the privilege of running an IPv6-only network* — the exact compromise the product exists to refuse. No platform will carry our packets, so we run our own machines. That is the founding grievance of the product, demonstrated on ourselves.

Deployment tooling is deliberately deferred: [Komodo](https://komo.do) when we have a second server, and not one day earlier. Adopting a deployment system before there is anything deployed is overhead arriving dressed as a solution to itself.
